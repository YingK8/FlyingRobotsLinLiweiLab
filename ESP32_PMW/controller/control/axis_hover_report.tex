\begin{abstract}
We design a hover controller for the magnetically spun rotor. It steers the rotation axis of the
coil field with the \texttt{vec=} command (\texttt{lib/VectorField}) to set the thrust direction,
and it sets the field frequency with \texttt{freq=} to set the thrust magnitude. A PID on attitude
is correct in spirit, but the attitude plant is \emph{gyroscopic}: the rotor axis precesses about
the field axis, with a phase of about $77^\circ$. A PID that tilts the field toward the wanted axis
therefore drives the axis sideways. We write the plant in complex form, cancel the precession phase
with an identified complex gain, and close the loop as a cascade PD with a disturbance observer. The
observer predicts across the measured latency by rewinding and replaying the logged commands. We give
a regression that identifies the commanded-to-actual axis map from flight video. We then prove
robust Schur stability for 17--33\,ms of latency over an explicit box of precession, gain and thrust
errors, and separately over a line of residual axis leak. The proof uses a structured small-gain
bound, exact $H_\infty$ norms and branch-and-bound over the parameters. The joint four-parameter box
is supported by exact eigenvalues at its corners and at 3000 random points, but it is not proven. We also bound the frequency excursion, so that the loop stays in a band far from the 190\,Hz
coil resonance, and we show with an energy argument that the commanded frequency slew cannot break
phase lock. The main negative result is that the residual, position-dependent field-axis error
(the ``axis leak'') sets the minimum lateral bandwidth. A slow, phase-robust design is destroyed by
a leak of only $0.02^\circ$/mm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Verdict: is this the right way to hover?}

\textbf{Yes, with three changes to the plain-PID idea.} All three are forced by the physics below.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Attitude cannot be a PID on the tilt error.} The rotor axis obeys
  $\dot\sigma=\lambda(\nu-\sigma)$ with complex $\lambda=\Omega_p e^{\jj\psi}$ and $\psi\approx-77^\circ$
  (\S\ref{sec:model}). The tilt is a first-order, \emph{rotating} response to the field tilt, not a
  double integrator. A D term on attitude adds nothing. Feeding back the tilt error with a real gain
  makes the axis spiral once $|\psi|$ approaches $90^\circ$. The fix is one complex multiply: command
  $\nu=\sigma+(k/\hat\lambda)(\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}-\sigma)$, so that the nominal closed attitude loop
  is $\dot\sigma=k(\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}-\sigma)$ with real $k$.
\item \textbf{The integral terms are replaced by bias states in the observer.} A plain integrator
  overshot by 115\,mm when the hover frequency was mis-estimated by 6\% (\S\ref{sec:negative}). An
  estimated thrust bias and an estimated tilt bias give the same zero steady-state error for constant
  disturbances, and they act at observer speed.
\item \textbf{The lateral bandwidth has a floor, set by the axis leak.} When the robot is off-axis,
  the field axis tilts with position. Whatever part \texttt{vec=}'s geometry compensation does not
  remove acts as positive position feedback. The design tolerates
  $|\kappa|\le 0.004$\,rad/mm ($0.23^\circ$/mm, about 16\% of the modelled $1.4^\circ$/mm) and
  needs the precession estimate to within $\times[0.7,1.4]$ and $\pm15^\circ$. Both numbers come from
  identification, so \textbf{identification is not optional}.
\end{enumerate}
LQR or MPC on the same model (\texttt{design\_hover\_lqr.py}, \texttt{LLM/design/spatial\_mpc.py})
would give similar gains. The cascade is preferred because each gain has a physical meaning, the
law is three complex multiply-adds, and it can be certified by the method below. A frequency-only
altitude loop, decoupled from attitude, is correct to first order. The coupling is second order in
tilt and bounded in \S\ref{sec:coupling}.

\section{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}
All lateral quantities are complex numbers. For a horizontal vector $(a_x,a_y)$ we write
$a=a_x+\jj a_y\in\C$. A rotation by $\phi$ is multiplication by $e^{\jj\phi}$.
$\arg z=\operatorname{atan2}(\Im z,\Re z)\in(-\pi,\pi]$, where $\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$ is the
angle of the point $(x,y)$ measured from the $+x$ axis. $\norm{\cdot}$ is the spectral norm and
$\norm{\cdot}_F$ the Frobenius norm. $\rho(A)$ is the spectral radius. $A^H$ is the conjugate
transpose. $\det(M)$ is the determinant. Time steps are indexed $k\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$.

\section{Plant}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Rotor axis}
The cycle-averaged torque of the rotating field on the spinning magnet has an aligning component
perpendicular to the spin. Acting on the angular momentum $L=I\omega$, this component turns the
spin axis \emph{perpendicular} to both the spin axis and the field axis, which is precession. Slip
and drag add a component toward the field axis. In tilt coordinates this gives
\begin{equation}
\dot\sigma=\lambda\,(\nu-\sigma),\qquad \lambda=\Omega_p e^{\jj\psi}.\label{eq:precession}
\end{equation}
Pure precession is $|\psi|=90^\circ$, and pure alignment is $\psi=0$. The multi-coil plant in
\texttt{LLM/design/spatial\_model.py}, with the axis re-allocated each step, has the eigenvalues
$-0.78\pm 3.43\jj$\,rad/s. The seed is therefore $\lambda_0=3.52\,e^{-\jj 77.2^\circ}$\,rad/s, with
the sign of $\psi$ set by the spin sense. The seed is a model value and must be measured
(\S\ref{sec:identify}).

\subsection{Field axis and the leak}
\texttt{vec=x:y:z:q:b} solves for duty and phase so that the field at the commanded point rotates
about $R(q)\uv z$ (\texttt{theory.md} \S28). The true axis differs from the commanded one through
channel gain and phase trims, the coil-to-camera registration, and model error. We write
\begin{equation}
\nu=a\,\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}}+c+\kappa\,w,\label{eq:fieldmap}
\end{equation}
where $a\in\C$ absorbs gain and yaw, $c\in\C$ is a constant offset (it includes the robot's crooked
$1.6^\circ$ seat), and $\kappa$ is the residual dependence on position. Without compensation the
field axis tilts by $1.4^\circ$/mm off-axis (\texttt{spatial\_model}), and this is the open-loop
divergence. The controller sends the \emph{predicted robot position} as the \texttt{vec=} point, so
VectorField removes most of the dependence, and $\kappa$ is what remains.

\subsection{Translation}
Thrust is $mg\beta$ along the rotor axis. For small tilt,
\begin{equation}
\dot w=v,\qquad \dot v=g\beta\,\sigma,\qquad \ddot z=g\Big(\beta\cos|\sigma|-1\Big),\qquad
\beta=\big(f/f_h\big)^2 .\label{eq:translation}
\end{equation}
The measured $f_h\approx101$\,Hz (\texttt{theory.md} 18.17), and the $f^2$ law breaks down above
about 110\,Hz. We therefore keep $f\in[85,115]$\,Hz, which is $\ge75$\,Hz below the coil resonance.

\subsection{Model used by the controller}
The lateral state is $x=(w,v,\sigma,b_\sigma)\in\C^4$, where $b_\sigma$ is a constant tilt bias. The
input is $u=\nu$, and the measurements are $y=(w,\sigma)$:
\begin{equation}
\dot x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\0&0&g&0\\ \hat\lambda\hat\kappa&0&-\hat\lambda&\hat\lambda\\0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}x
+\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\ \hat\lambda\\0\end{pmatrix}u .\label{eq:lateral}
\end{equation}
The vertical state is $(z,\dot z,b_z)\in\R^3$ with input $a_z$ (mm/s$^2$), $\ddot z=a_z+b_z$, and
measurement $z$. Both models are discretised exactly (zero-order hold, block matrix exponential;
\texttt{discretised}).

\section{Controller}
\label{sec:controller}

\subsection{Lateral: complex cascade PD}
Inner loop: $u=\sigma+(k/\hat\lambda)(\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}-\sigma)-b_\sigma-\hat\kappa w$, which gives
$\dot\sigma=k(\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}-\sigma)$ when the model is exact. Outer loop:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}=-(K_p w+K_d v)/g$. The characteristic polynomial is
\[
s^2(s+k)+k(K_d s+K_p)=s^3+k s^2+kK_d s+kK_p ,
\]
so the three poles $p_1,p_2,p_3$ fix $k=c_1$, $K_d=c_2/c_1$ and $K_p=c_3/c_1$, where
$\prod_{i=1}^{3}(s-p_i)=s^3+c_1s^2+c_2s+c_3$ (\texttt{lateral\_feedback}). The shipped poles are
$(-12,\,-4\pm4\jj)$\,rad/s. The outer natural frequency is $5.7$\,rad/s ($0.9$\,Hz), and
\S\ref{sec:negative} explains why it cannot be lower. The state-feedback row is
\[
K=\Big(\tfrac{c_3}{\hat\lambda g}+\hat\kappa,\ \tfrac{c_2}{\hat\lambda g},\ \tfrac{c_1}{\hat\lambda}-1,\ 1\Big),\qquad u=-K\hat x .
\]
The command is clamped to $|u|\le0.35$\,rad. It is sent as
$\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}}=(u-\hat c)/\hat a$, as the axis $\uv n\propto(\Re\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}},\Im\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}},1)$
and the quaternion $q=\big(1+n_z,\,-n_y,\,n_x,\,0\big)/\norm{\cdot}$. This is the minimal rotation
$\uv z\to\uv n$, so the twist about the axis is identically zero and never jumps (\texttt{theory.md}
\S28.5).

\subsection{Vertical: PD on acceleration, mapped to frequency}
$a_z=-K_pz-K_d\dot z-b_z$, with poles $-2.22\pm2.22\jj$\,rad/s. The frequency that delivers $a_z$ is
\[
f=\hat f_h\sqrt{1+a_z/g},
\]
clamped to the band $[85,115]$\,Hz and then slew-limited to $5$\,Hz/s. The \emph{achieved}
acceleration $g\big((f/\hat f_h)^2-1\big)$ is fed back into the observer, so saturation cannot wind
up the bias.

\subsection{Latency: rewind and replay}
\label{sec:latency}
A pose stamped at host receipt $t_{\mathrm{rx}}$ was exposed at
$t_{\mathrm{sh}}=t_{\mathrm{rx}}-0.022$\,s (\texttt{constants.CAMERA\_LATENCY\_S}, measured from LED
edges). At step $k$ the controller holds its history $\{(\hat x_j,u_j)\}_{j=k-12}^{k-1}$. A
measurement with age $\hat d=\operatorname{round}((t_k-t_{\mathrm{sh}})/T)$ is handled in two steps.
First it corrects the stored estimate $\hat x_{k-\hat d}\leftarrow\hat x_{k-\hat d}+L(y-C\hat x_{k-\hat d})$.
Then it replays $\hat x_{j+1}=\Phi\hat x_j+\Gamma u_j$ for $j=k-\hat d,\dots,k-1$, overwriting the
history (\texttt{DelayedLoop.correct}). This is a Kalman filter on the delayed time line plus an exact
model predictor, and it is the feed-forward that interpolating between pose estimates cannot give:
interpolation adds no information and adds a sample of lag, while the replay uses the commands that
were actually applied. $L$ is the steady-state Kalman gain from the discrete Riccati equation.
Its noise levels are: position $0.034$\,mm (\texttt{noise\_model.json}); tilt $0.1$\,rad, on purpose
25$\times$ the measured $0.0039$ (\S\ref{sec:negative}); acceleration $30$\,mm/s$^2$; tilt rate
$0.3$\,rad/s; bias drift $0.01$\,rad/s and $300$\,mm/s$^3$.

\section{Identifying the commanded-to-actual axis map from video}
\label{sec:identify}
Combining \eqref{eq:precession} and \eqref{eq:fieldmap} gives a model that is linear in its unknowns:
\begin{equation}
\dot\sigma=\underbrace{\lambda a}_{\alpha}\,\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}}\;\underbrace{-\lambda}_{\beta_\sigma}\,\sigma
\;+\;\underbrace{\lambda\kappa}_{\delta}\,w\;+\;\underbrace{\lambda c}_{\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Given the measured axis $\sigma(t)$, offset $w(t)$ and the held command $\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)$, one
complex least-squares solve returns $(\alpha,\beta_\sigma,\delta,\gamma)$, and then $\hat\lambda=-\beta_\sigma$,
$\hat a=\alpha/\hat\lambda$, $\hat\kappa=\delta/\hat\lambda$ and $\hat c=\gamma/\hat\lambda$
(\texttt{fit\_axis\_response}). $\dot\sigma$ and $\sigma$ come from a quadratic Savitzky--Golay
filter (15 samples) on a uniform resample. The coil poses enter only through $\hat a$, $\hat c$
and the \texttt{vec=} point offset. If the registration is wrong, $\hat a$ and $\hat c$ absorb the
error.

\paragraph{Experiment.} \texttt{fly(law="axis", identify\_axis=True, ...)} holds altitude closed-loop
and sends open-loop tilt steps of $0.05$\,rad: zero, then eight azimuths, each for $1.5$\,s, with
zero in between. It writes \texttt{<run>\_axis.npz}. Then run
\texttt{python controller/control/axis\_hover.py fit <run>\_axis.npz response.json} and fly with
\texttt{axis\_response="response.json"}. The runner refuses \texttt{law="axis"} without a fitted
response.

\paragraph{Cautions.} The video axis is unsigned, so the fit pins $n_z>0$. Spin rate and spin sense
are not observable above about 26\,Hz (blade strobe), so the sign of $\psi$ is a fit output, not an
input. Takes with dropped frames are mistimed and must be excluded. Stay clear of the singular shell
at $z\approx20.8\pm1.5$\,mm above the coils, where $a\to0$; the controller refuses hover points there.
If the rod constrains the tilt on the stand, run the steps in free hover with the seed and a small
amplitude instead. The same regression works on closed-loop flight logs, since $\nu_{\mathrm{cmd}}$
is logged.

\section{Stability certificate}
\label{sec:proof}

\subsection{The closed loop as an exact linear recursion}
Let the true lateral plant (\eqref{eq:precession}--\eqref{eq:translation}, bias removed, since a
constant disturbance does not affect stability) have the ZOH step $[\Phi_p\ \Gamma_p](\theta)$ for
the parameters
$\theta=(|\lambda/\lambda_0|,\ \arg(\lambda/\lambda_0),\ \beta,\ \kappa)$. The augmented state is
\[
\zeta_k=\big(x^p_k,\,x^p_{k-1},\dots,x^p_{k-d};\ e_k;\ u_{k-\hat d},\dots,u_{k-1}\big),
\]
where $e_k$ is the stored estimate in the slot that the incoming measurement corrects. One step of
\texttt{DelayedLoop} is then exactly
\[
\zeta_{k+1}=\mathcal A(\theta)\zeta_k,\qquad \mathcal A(\theta)=\mathcal A_0+E\,[\Phi_p\ \Gamma_p](\theta)\,F ,
\]
with $E$ inserting the new plant state and $F$ extracting $(x^p_k,u_k)$ (\texttt{closed\_loop}). The
map is affine in the plant step. That structure is what makes the proof tractable.

\subsection{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let the true delay be $d\in\{2,3,4\}$ steps ($16.7$--$33.3$\,ms), with $\hat d=3$ assumed. Then
$\rho(\mathcal A(\theta))<1$ for every constant $\theta$ in each of the following sets:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\Theta_1=[0.7,1.4]\times[-15^\circ,15^\circ]\times[0.77,1.26]\times\{0\}$, which covers
  precession gain, precession phase and thrust ratio with the leak identified and cancelled;
\item $\Theta_2=\{1\}\times\{0\}\times\{1\}\times[-0.004,0.004]$\,rad/mm, which covers the residual
  leak at the nominal precession.
\end{enumerate}
The vertical loop is Schur stable for every $\beta\in[0.5,2]$ and $d\in\{2,3,4\}$, so $f_h$ may lie
anywhere in $[71,143]$\,Hz around the assumed $101$\,Hz.
\end{theorem}
The joint box $\Theta_1\times[-0.004,0.004]$ is \emph{not} proven. Covering it would take about
$7\times10^5$ cells, roughly 30\,h, because the small-gain bound treats the real parameter $\kappa$ as
if it were complex. The worst spectral radius over its $2^4$ corners and 3000 uniform samples, at all
three delays, is \evidenceRadius.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (one stable point).} At the centre $\theta_c$ of a cell, $\rho(\mathcal A(\theta_c))<1$
is checked directly.

\emph{Step 2 (no eigenvalue crosses the unit circle).} By continuity of the eigenvalues, stability
holds on a connected cell if $\det(zI-\mathcal A(\theta))\neq0$ for all $|z|=1$ and all $\theta$ in
the cell. Write $\mathcal A(\theta)=\mathcal A_c+E\Delta F$ with
$\Delta=[\Phi_p\Gamma_p](\theta)-[\Phi_p\Gamma_p](\theta_c)$ and
$G(z)=F(zI-\mathcal A_c)^{-1}E$. Since $zI-\mathcal A=(zI-\mathcal A_c)(I-(zI-\mathcal A_c)^{-1}E\Delta F)$,
it suffices that $\sup_{|z|=1}\norm{G(z)\Delta}<1$, which is the small-gain condition.

\emph{Step 3 (structured bound on $\Delta$).} Let $X(\theta)=T\,D M(\theta)D^{-1}$ be the scaled
generator of the ZOH block, with $D$ the diagonal scaling $(|\lambda_0|^2/g,\ |\lambda_0|/g,\ 1,\ 1)$.
The scaling is a similarity, so it leaves the stability question unchanged and balances
mm against rad. With $\mathrm{L}_X(\cdot)$ the Fréchet derivative of $\exp$ at $X_c$, and
$Y=X(\theta)-X_c=\sum_i(\theta_i-\theta_{c,i})\partial_iX+Y_2$,
\[
\Delta=\sum_i(\theta_i-\theta_{c,i})\,\mathrm{L}_X(\partial_iX)+\mathrm{L}_X(Y_2)+R,\qquad
\norm{R}\le\tfrac12\norm{Y}^2e^{\norm{X_c}+\norm Y}.
\]
$\mathrm{L}_X(\partial_iX)$ is the upper-right block of $\exp\begin{psmallmatrix}X_c&\partial_iX\\0&X_c\end{psmallmatrix}$.
$Y_2$ collects the curvature of $\lambda=\lambda_0 re^{\jj\phi}$ and of the product $\lambda\kappa$,
bounded entrywise by
$|\lambda_0|(h_rh_\phi+\tfrac12 r_{\max}h_\phi^2)$ and its product rule (\texttt{LateralUncertainty.curvature}).
Hence
\[
\sup_{|z|=1}\norm{G\Delta}\le\sum_i h_i\,\norm{G\,\mathrm{L}_X(\partial_iX)}_\infty
+\norm{G}_\infty\Big(\norm{Y_2}e^{\norm{X_c}}+\tfrac12\norm Y^2e^{\norm{X_c}+\norm Y}\Big),
\]
where $h_i$ are the cell half-widths. The certificate requires the right-hand side to be $<1$.

\emph{Step 4 (exact $H_\infty$ norms).} Each $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$ is computed with the Cayley transform
$z=(1+s)/(1-s)$, which maps the unit circle onto the imaginary axis and preserves the peak gain. It is
followed by the Hamiltonian test (Boyd--Balakrishnan): $\gamma>\norm{G}_\infty$ if and only if the
Hamiltonian $H(\gamma)$ has no imaginary eigenvalue. Bisection keeps an upper end $\gamma$ at which
no crossing is found (\texttt{peak\_gain}). A dense unit-circle grid agrees with it to $0.5\%$ and
sits on the safe side.

\emph{Step 5 (cover).} Each set is split along its widest normalised side until every cell satisfies
Steps 1--3 (\texttt{certify}). For $d=2,3,4$ the run needed:
$\Theta_1$: \certLateralC, \certLateralD, \certLateralE\ cells; $\Theta_2$: \certLeakC, \certLeakD,
\certLeakE\ cells; vertical: \certVerticalC, \certVerticalD, \certVerticalE\ cell(s).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the theorem does not cover.} (i) The arithmetic is IEEE double, not interval
arithmetic, so this is a computer-assisted proof up to rounding. The reported slacks exceed rounding
by many orders of magnitude, but they are not formally enclosed. (ii) Parameters are frozen. Slow
variation is covered by the usual slowly-varying argument; fast switching, such as frame dropouts
(a missing correction), is not. (iii) The plant is the linearisation; saturation is handled in
\S\ref{sec:band} and phase lock in \S\ref{sec:lock}. (iv) The leak is certified on its own, not jointly with
the other three errors, and it is modelled as isotropic, a real $\kappa$ along $w$. An anisotropic or
rotated leak (a general real $2\times2$ map) needs another dimension. A real-parameter ($\mu$) bound
would remove the complex-$\kappa$ conservatism and is the natural next step. (v) The pose filter that feeds \texttt{tick.pose} is not in the model; at $500$\,mm/s$^2$
process noise it is nearly transparent at $1$\,Hz.

\subsection{Staying in the band: no saturation for small errors}
\label{sec:band}
For the nominal loop we iterate $\zeta_{k+1}=\mathcal A\zeta_k$ from a consistent initial offset (plant
and estimate agree) until it decays below $10^{-6}$. We record the peak field-tilt command and the
peak $|a_z|$ and $|\Delta a_z|$ per mm of initial offset. By linearity:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a lateral offset of up to \regionLateralMm\,mm never reaches the $0.35$\,rad tilt clamp;
\item a height error of up to \regionHeightBandMm\,mm never leaves $[85,115]$\,Hz, and up to
  \regionHeightSlewMm\,mm never meets the $5$\,Hz/s slew limit;
\item the frequency moves by at most \regionHzPerMm\,Hz per mm of height error.
\end{itemize}
The loop therefore never commands within $75$\,Hz of the coil resonance. In simulation the steady
frequency ripple is $0.34$\,Hz peak-to-peak.

\subsection{Phase lock under the frequency slew}
\label{sec:lock}
\begin{lemma}
Let the lag $\delta$ obey $I\ddot\delta=\tau(\ell-\sin\delta)$ with load
$\ell=(k_{\mathrm{drag}}f^2+2\pi I\dot f)/\tau$, and let $\dot f$ switch from $-\dot f_{\max}$ to
$+\dot f_{\max}$ (the worst slew reversal). Lock is kept if
$U(\pi-\arcsin\ell_+)>U(\arcsin\ell_-)$, with $U(\delta)=-\cos\delta-\ell_+\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$U$ is the potential of the post-switch dynamics. The rotor starts at rest relative to the old
equilibrium $\arcsin\ell_-$ with energy $U(\arcsin\ell_-)$. The separatrix passes through the saddle
$\pi-\arcsin\ell_+$. With no damping (a conservative assumption) the orbit is bounded inside the
lock basin if and only if its energy is below the saddle.
\end{proof}
With \texttt{z\_track.TorqueLimits} at $f_h=101$\,Hz, $\dot f_{\max}=5$\,Hz/s and every $f$ in the
band, the margin is \lockMargin\ ($>0$). It stays positive down to \lockTorqueScale$\times$ the
modelled peak torque. The slew term is negligible: $2\pi I\dot f_{\max}\approx 0.5\%$ of the peak
torque. What matters is the drag at the top of the band. The modelled torque depends on the stale
$F_{\mathrm{STEPOUT}}=225$\,Hz, so this lemma says how wrong the step-out torque may be (up to a
factor $1/$\lockTorqueScale) before the band must shrink.

\subsection{Coupling between attitude and altitude}
\label{sec:coupling}
Tilt reduces vertical thrust by $g\beta(1-\cos|\sigma|)\le g\beta|\sigma|^2/2$. At the $0.35$\,rad
clamp this is $\le6\%\,g$; at the $\le0.02$\,rad of steady hover it is $0.02\%\,g$. The vertical loop is
exponentially stable and its bias state absorbs the constant part. The remainder is quadratic in a
state that decays exponentially, so the cascade is locally exponentially stable. Frequency changes
$\beta$ in the lateral loop, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} covers $\beta\in[0.77,1.26]$, which is
$f\in[89,113]$\,Hz at $f_h=101$.

\paragraph{Is the 120\,Hz requirement met?} The native tracker (C++ capture, interleaved pairing)
delivers 221--240 poses/s on the live rig with zero lost out of 34\,700. The hover runner, however,
was calling \texttt{stereo\_frames} without \texttt{tracker=True}, and so flew on the Python path at
65--100\,Hz. \textbf{Changed:} \texttt{RunConfig.tracker=True} by default. The controller runs at a fixed
$120$\,Hz on the newest pose, which leaves $1.8\times$ headroom.

\paragraph{The largest latency term was not compensated.} \texttt{tick.t} is the host receipt time,
not the shutter time, so the existing predictor propagated only about 3\,ms. \textbf{Changed:} both the
LQR path and the axis law now use $t_{\mathrm{sh}}=t_{\mathrm{rx}}-22$\,ms. The constant was moved to
\texttt{control/constants.py} and is shared with the video alignment.

\paragraph{Compute.} \textbf{Changed:} \texttt{live\_viz} no longer calls \texttt{trk.stats()} on every pose.
That call copies up to 20\,000 skew samples and sorts them under the GIL; it now runs once a second.
\textbf{Left as measured opportunities} (each needs a native rebuild and a parity run):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \texttt{segment()} runs absdiff, thresholds, morphology and connected components on the full
  frame even when the ROI is known. It is 82\% of the 3.3\,ms. An ROI window would roughly halve
  the cost per pose.
\item a full-frame \texttt{float32} buffer is allocated per call in \texttt{ring\_weight}, the structuring
  element is rebuilt per call, and there are five full-frame passes in \texttt{RunningPlate::update};
\item a \texttt{std::thread} is created per pose when both views are fresh (\texttt{stereo.cpp:240});
\item both frames are deep-cloned per worker step, and \texttt{result\_py} copies about 1\,MB of masks into Python per pose;
\item \texttt{best\_format} may select MJPEG. A raw (YUYV/GREY) mode would remove the host decode,
  about 6\,ms of CPU, and part of the 22\,ms latency, if the USB bandwidth allows it at 640$\times$400.
\end{enumerate}
None of these is needed for hover at 120\,Hz. The latency in item 5 is the only one that would move
the certificate: it lowers $d$.

\paragraph{Why not a faster control clock?} The loop poles are at most $12$\,rad/s. At $T=1/120$\,s,
ZOH costs $\omega T/2$ of phase: $1.4^\circ$ at the $5.7$\,rad/s outer crossover and $2.9^\circ$ at the
inner pole. A 500\,Hz clock would recover about $2^\circ$ of that, consistent with \texttt{theory.md}
19.14. The outer loop sits at $0.9$\,Hz, just above the old $0.8$\,Hz rule. That rule came from one
uncompensated frame of delay on the az/mag plant; here the delay is predicted across and certified.

\section{Negative results}
\label{sec:negative}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Integral action overshoots.} PID with integrators, started mid-air with $f_h$ off by
  6\%, climbed 115\,mm before the integrator caught up. Replaced by bias states.
\item \textbf{Trusting the tilt measurement costs a millimetre.} A tilt error $e$ produces a lateral
  acceleration $ge$, and $g/\omega^2\approx 3400$\,mm/rad at $0.27$\,Hz. With the measured
  $0.0039$\,rad per frame, the hover wandered by about 1\,mm RMS whatever the process noise. Weighting
  the tilt measurement at $0.1$\,rad lets position, which is $g$-exact, carry the tilt estimate.
\item \textbf{The slow, phase-robust design fails on the leak.} Poles $(-6,-1.2\pm1.2\jj)$ tolerate
  $\pm40^\circ$ of precession phase and a $\times[0.5,2]$ gain error. They tolerate only
  $|\kappa|\le3.6\times10^{-4}$\,rad/mm ($0.02^\circ$/mm), because the outer stiffness
  $K_p/g=2\times10^{-4}$\,rad/mm is 35$\times$ smaller than a leak of 30\% of the modelled
  $1.4^\circ$/mm. Trade-off (phase
  tolerance at gains $[0.7,1.4]$, leak tolerance):
  \begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lcc}
  \toprule poles (rad/s) & phase & $|\kappa|_{\max}$ (rad/mm) \\ \midrule
  $-9,\ -3\pm3\jj$ & $30^\circ$ & 0.0031 \\
  $-12,\ -4\pm4\jj$ (shipped) & $20^\circ$ & 0.0059 \\
  $-15,\ -5\pm5\jj$ & $10^\circ$ & 0.0112 \\ \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\end{center}
  The certified box uses $15^\circ$ and $0.004$ to leave room at the corners.
\item \textbf{A common quadratic Lyapunov function and an unstructured small gain are too
  conservative.} With $P$ from $\mathcal A^HP\mathcal A-P=-I$, the invariant ellipsoid admitted only
  $0.006$\,mm. The unstructured complex stability radius ($8\times10^{-6}$ before scaling,
  $10^{-3}$ after) needed millions of cells. The per-direction $H_\infty$ bound of Step 3 is about
  100$\times$ tighter.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Unmeasured, in order of what blocks hover}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item $\lambda$ (rate and phase, sign by spin sense), $a$, $c$ and $\kappa$: run the identification take.
\item The coil-to-camera registration. The holder survey is unconfirmed; this report assumes the
  coil pose is known (\texttt{coil\_offset\_mm}, \texttt{coil\_yaw\_deg}).
\item $f_h$ to $\pm7\%$. It is certified over a much wider range, but the band $[85,115]$ assumes it.
\item The step-out torque. The lemma tolerates down to \lockTorqueScale$\times$ the model value.
\item The singular-shell height. The controller refuses $19.3$--$22.3$\,mm; the true height must be measured.
\item The VectorField \texttt{scale=} reply is not yet read back. If it drops below 1, the field is
  weaker than commanded. Land on it once a flight shows it happening.
\end{enumerate}
