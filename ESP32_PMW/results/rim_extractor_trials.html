The metric was measuring the wrong thing

Seven ellipse extractors, two stereo flights. The headline result is not which one won — it is that the two ways of scoring them disagree, and the one this project has been tuned on is the one that is wrong.

flights 2026-08-26_184830, 2026-08-26_184940 stereo frames 171 + 235 threshold 180accept gate 25.5 mm

Extraction accuracy and stereo agreement are anti-correlated

On 2026-08-26_184940, iterative contour pruning is the most accurate extractor of the seven — 2.0 px median centre error against the shipped baseline's 3.6 px — and scores half the solve rate on the same frames, 23.0% against 43.8%. On 2026-08-26_184830 the ordering reverses on both metrics.

A more accurate ellipse cannot make the rig geometry worse. What it can do is stop compensating for it: with a wrong extrinsic, a biased ellipse in one view partially cancels the rig error, and a cleaner one exposes it. Cross-view discrepancy is therefore not ranking extractors here — it is ranking how each method's bias happens to interact with a bad calibration.

This applies to every millimetre figure in this report, and to the tuning done against them. RING_BAND = 0.5 in pose/segment.py was chosen on cross-view discrepancy and needs re-deriving once the rig is re-solved.

Two negative results worth keeping

Selecting on fit error instead of size does not work. Fit error has no support term, so its minimum is always the smallest candidate: against convex hulls it picks the roundest blob (a rim and a solid disc both score 0.0004), against mask pixels it picks the thinnest arc, since an ellipse follows a short arc almost exactly. Measured at 8% and 7% solved against 45% and 44%. Gating on error and breaking ties on inlier count improves monotonically as the gate is widened, and is best with the gate off entirely.

Normalising pruning's objective by perimeter changes nothing. Inlier count scales with circumference, so a large ellipse threading scattered blobs can outscore the true rim — a real effect, visible as 760–803 px fits where the rim is 450–500. Dividing by perimeter moved 2026-08-26_184940 from 23.0% to 25.1%, and weighting by angular coverage was no better. Not the dominant cause.

Ground truth has a failure mode too

Consensus voting breaks when the majority shares a bias. On 2026-08-26_184830 view A frame 420 no ground truth was found at all: only pruning (463×278) and per-contour (480×289) agreed, and those two were the visually correct pair. The baseline returned 607×502 after absorbing shadow blobs, Otsu 792×504, RANSAC 767×454 — three methods that consume the same threshold mask and fail together.

Consensus was available on 70% of views. The frames it cannot label are exactly the hard ones, so the centre-error figures above are a floor on the difference between methods rather than a full measure of it.

The methods

prune: iterative rejection

Most accurate

Start from every blob, fit, then drop the smallest remaining contour and refit, keeping whichever set best explains the segmentation.

The most consistent extractor measured: worst-case 2.7 px centre error across both flights against the baseline's 3.6 px, and the best on 184940 at 2.0 px. Growing a group from an anchor can only add blobs, so scenery once absorbed can never be shed; pruning is the only tested rule that can reject it.

What to do next

Re-solve the extrinsic (python calib/calibrate.py). A sound rig on this bench reaches a 2.2 mm cross-view floor with a sharp radius minimum at 10.4 mm; neither of these flights produces that minimum at any threshold, and every method here bottoms out between 25 and 38 mm. Once the geometry is right the two metrics should agree again — and at that point the extractor comparison can be re-run and settled honestly, along with RING_BAND.

Cross-view discrepancy: each camera back-projects its own rim ellipse to a 3-D position; the two answers agree only when both extraction and rig geometry are right. Needs no ground truth — which is why it was used, and why its failure mode here was not obvious. Shipped pipeline in pose/segment.py. The six alternatives were run from a scratch harness and are not in the tree.
